# Robustness evaluation of the landmark classifier

Everything here is regenerated by `python -m ml.robustness.run_eval` and `python -m ml.robustness.report`
(raw tables: [`ml/results/robustness/summary.md`](../ml/results/robustness/summary.md)).
All numbers are mean ± std over 3 seeds (42, 1, 2) of the same CNN (Conv1D64-Conv1D128-Dense256, 589,852
parameters) trained on raw MediaPipe landmarks with a leakage-aware block split. For each seed every model variant uses
the identical train/test split.

## Threat model
White-box adversary who can add a perturbation to the 63-d landmark vector fed to the classifier, bounded in
L_inf or L2 (units: fraction of the image width; 0.01 = 1 % of the width). This models a corrupted or compromised
landmark stage. It is **not** a physical or pixel-space attack on the camera image, and MediaPipe itself is **not** attacked.
Attacks: FGSM and PGD (20 steps, random start, step 2.5·ε/20), on a fixed random subset of 5,000 block-test samples
(about ±0.6 points of sampling error). Training variants: baseline; **aug** (random roll ±30°, tilt ±20°, scale ±15 %,
shift ±0.1, jitter σ=0.005 about the wrist); **PGD-AT** (L_inf ε=0.01, 5 steps, random start); **aug + PGD-AT**.

## Results

### 1. Adversarial robustness (PGD L_inf, accuracy %)
| model | clean | ε=0.005 | ε=0.01 | ε=0.02 | ε=0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 96.7 ± 0.5 | 84.5 ± 0.4 | **60.8 ± 1.2** | 18.3 ± 2.4 | 0.6 ± 0.3 |
| augmentation | 96.6 ± 0.3 | 85.0 ± 1.0 | 60.3 ± 3.1 | 20.6 ± 3.5 | 1.0 ± 0.4 |
| PGD adversarial training | 96.5 ± 0.3 | 93.6 ± 0.6 | **88.7 ± 0.8** | 71.0 ± 1.2 | 9.7 ± 0.9 |
| augmentation + PGD-AT | 95.9 ± 0.3 | 92.9 ± 1.0 | 88.2 ± 0.5 | 70.2 ± 1.2 | 12.0 ± 2.2 |

* The undefended model loses a third of its accuracy at ε=0.01 and is near zero at ε=0.05.
* PGD adversarial training (trained at ε=0.01) lifts accuracy at ε=0.01 from 60.8 % to 88.7 % at no measurable clean cost
  (96.7 → 96.5, within noise), and keeps 71 % at twice the training budget. It does **not** make the model robust at large budgets (9.7 % at ε=0.05).
* Augmentation gives no adversarial robustness. L2 attacks and FGSM show the same ordering (see summary.md).
* Sanity checks: PGD ≤ FGSM at every ε, accuracy falls monotonically to ≈0 at large ε, and L2 results agree with L_inf.
  This is evidence against gradient masking but **not a proof**: only PGD-20 was used (no restarts, no adaptive or
  black-box attacks such as AutoAttack). Treat the numbers as upper bounds on true robustness.

### 2. Natural perturbations (block-test, accuracy %)
| perturbation | baseline | augmentation | PGD-AT | aug + PGD-AT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in-plane rotation 20° | 89.4 ± 1.2 | 95.8 ± 0.3 | 88.9 ± 1.5 | 94.4 ± 1.0 |
| in-plane rotation 30° | 68.9 ± 1.8 | 94.2 ± 0.5 | 69.7 ± 0.9 | 91.9 ± 0.9 |
| in-plane rotation 45° | 33.7 ± 0.5 | 86.9 ± 1.2 | 35.1 ± 1.7 | 83.5 ± 1.3 |
| tilt 20° (x/y axes) | 94.0 ± 0.4 | 96.4 ± 0.3 | 95.7 ± 0.3 | 95.7 ± 0.4 |
| jitter σ=0.02 | 93.0 ± 0.2 | 92.7 ± 0.2 | 94.1 ± 0.3 | 93.5 ± 0.3 |
| 3 landmarks dropped | 83.5 ± 0.2 | 80.9 ± 0.6 | 87.0 ± 0.2 | 84.7 ± 0.7 |
| scale 0.8 / 1.25, shift 0.1 | ≥ 96.4 | ≥ 96.4 | ≥ 95.8 | ≥ 95.1 |

The baseline is fragile to in-plane rotation (96 % → 34 % at 45°). Augmentation fixes that (87 %), but does not help against
adversarial attacks; adversarial training helps against attacks but not rotation. **The two defences are complementary**, and
combining them gives both (83.5 % at 45°, 88.2 % at PGD ε=0.01) for a cost of about 0.8 points of clean accuracy.
Rotations are applied in normalised image coordinates and ignore the image aspect ratio (approximate).

### 3. Held-out data from other sources (clean accuracy %)
| model | Kaggle block-test | 5 other signers (`asl_dataset`, n=1,286) | own webcam, 1 light (n=2,838) | own webcam, 3 lights (n=3,481) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 96.7 ± 0.5 | 71.1 ± 2.1 | 45.1 ± 1.5 | 51.1 ± 2.2 |
| augmentation | 96.6 ± 0.3 | 73.1 ± 0.7 | 50.3 ± 3.3 | 50.6 ± 2.1 |
| PGD-AT | 96.5 ± 0.3 | 75.5 ± 1.2 | 47.8 ± 3.1 | 53.8 ± 2.0 |
| aug + PGD-AT | 95.9 ± 0.3 | 78.1 ± 0.7 | 48.6 ± 1.6 | 50.8 ± 0.9 |

* The dominant problem is **distribution shift**, not attacks: accuracy falls from ≈97 % in-dataset to 45-78 % on other
  signers and on a real webcam.
* Defences give a modest, consistent gain on the 5-signer set (71 → 78 points for aug + PGD-AT) but **no reliable gain on the webcam
  sets** (differences are within 1-2 std over 3 seeds).
* Because rotation augmentation fixed rotation sensitivity but did **not** fix the webcam gap, rotation/tilt is not the main
  cause of the failure on real webcam data. The cause is still open (candidates: signer-specific hand shapes, the dataset's
  camera/pose conventions, different sign conventions for some letters, MediaPipe behaviour on this webcam).

## Limitations
* Webcam test sets come from **one person** in two lighting/position conditions: they measure robustness to lighting and
  setup, not generalisation across signers. The 5-signer set is small and cropped differently from the training data.
* The block split assumes Kaggle image numbers follow video order. Checked on the extracted landmarks: neighbouring ids are about
  10x closer than random same-class pairs (median landmark distance 0.08 vs 1.24) and similarity fades to random level by
  50-100 ids apart, so the ordering holds. The 25-id gap leaves some residual similarity at block edges, so a single seed
  (42) was re-run with a 100-id gap: 96.0 % vs 96.4 % with gap 25, within seed noise (std 0.45), so the headline is not
  inflated by the gap choice.
* Attack evaluation: PGD-20 on a 5,000-sample subset, landmark space only, white-box; no adaptive attacks, no certification.
* J and Z are motion letters evaluated as single frames.
* 3 seeds: differences below roughly one standard deviation should not be interpreted.
